The product

The working capital loan in the catalogue

The working capital loan is already a line among the financing products. It serves operating needs, between cash coming in and cash going out. The corporate and strategic financing page also places it with growth, acquisition, refinancing and debt consolidation.

On its own, it does not finance a machine, a truck or agricultural equipment. When the need is a named asset, the file starts with equipment financing.

What the catalogue says

  • Working capital loan
  • Tied to operating and growth projects
  • Separate from financing an asset

Two different needs

Operations, or a piece of equipment

An operating need

A working capital loan is aimed at the resources that keep the business running. Versa Capital prepares the file and presents it to its partners. No amount is shown before a written offer.

A need for an asset

An excavator, a truck, a trailer or agricultural equipment is financed as equipment, often by lease or by a term loan to purchase. That is not the role of this page.

Link with refinancing

Free up capacity, or finance operations

A business that already owns equipment can look at a sale-leaseback. That transaction starts from an asset. A working capital loan does not start from the sale of equipment. The two products answer different starting points.

The treatment of interest and principal depends on the use of the loan and on the situation of the business. Versa Capital does not give tax advice. An accountant or a tax advisor confirms the treatment before signing.

FAQ

Common questions

What is a working capital loan at Versa Capital?

It is a catalogue product for the operating needs of the business. It is separate from financing a piece of equipment.

Does it replace financing for a machine or a truck?

No. A specific asset is financed as equipment. A working capital loan does not stand in for that file.

Where does it sit relative to corporate financing?

The corporate and strategic financing page mentions it alongside growth, acquisitions, refinancing and debt consolidation.

How is it different from a sale-leaseback?

A sale-leaseback starts from equipment the business sells, then keeps using. A working capital loan does not start from that sale.

Is an amount shown on this page?

No. No amount is published. Only a written offer, after the file is reviewed, can contain one.

Is the interest deductible?

Versa Capital does not state that it is. The treatment depends on the use of the loan and on the situation of the business. An accountant or a tax advisor must confirm it.
