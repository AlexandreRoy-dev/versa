The need

Machinery the site already covers

This page is about construction equipment, excavation equipment and forestry equipment. Those are the assets Versa Capital already places in the Construction and Forestry sectors.

The file may be a lease, a term loan to purchase, or an operating lease. The choice follows the project, the business profile, liquidity, how long the asset will be held, and the long-term objectives.

What is at stake

  • Construction and excavation equipment
  • Forestry equipment
  • Access to more than 20 financial partners

Two ways to structure it

Buy the machine, or use it without a buyout

Loan or capital lease

A term loan to purchase finances the acquisition: the business is the buyer and repays a loan. A capital lease, a form of leasing, is structured so the equipment can be acquired at the end of the term, according to what is written in the contract.

Operating lease

An operating lease with no purchase guarantee covers the use of the machine during the contract. The business has no commitment to buy the asset at the end.

If the business already owns the machine

Start from a machine already owned

A machine that is already paid for can be the subject of refinancing or a sale-leaseback. The starting point is then equipment the business owns, not a machine still to be acquired.

The accounting treatment depends on the contract and on the framework used. Versa Capital does not give tax advice. An accountant or a tax advisor confirms the treatment before signing.

To remember

  • Construction, excavation and forestry
  • Financed purchase or a capital lease
  • Use with no purchase guarantee, if the contract says so

FAQ

Common questions

Which machinery does this page cover?

Construction equipment, excavation equipment and forestry equipment, the assets already named in the Construction and Forestry sectors.

Which structures can Versa Capital present?

A capital lease, a term loan to purchase, or an operating lease with no purchase guarantee. The structure that is kept is the one in the contract, after a comparison with partners.

How does a purchase differ from a lease with no buyout?

With a term loan, the business buys the machine and repays a loan. With an operating lease and no purchase guarantee, it uses the machine during the term, with no commitment to buy it at the end.

Can the financing of a machine the business already owns be reviewed?

Yes, through refinancing or a sale-leaseback, described on the page for that purpose. That file starts from an asset the business already owns.

Who prepares the file?

Versa Capital understands the project, structures it, presents it to more than 20 financial partners and compares the options with the business. One point of contact follows the file through funding.

Does financing a machine have one set tax treatment?

No, not in a uniform way. The treatment depends on the contract and on the situation of the business. An accountant or a tax advisor must confirm it before signing.
